How to check a used car before you buy: 12 things to look at

A used car can be a great buy or an expensive mistake, and the difference usually shows up in the first 30 minutes of looking. Use these checks at any dealer or private sale. Tick them off as you go; your progress stays on this device.

Outside the car

Walk around the car in daylight before anyone talks you through it. Look along each side at an angle: ripples, uneven gaps between panels and paint that doesn't quite match are signs of past bodywork.

Under the hood and inside

Ask the seller not to start the car before you arrive. A cold start tells you more than a warm one: listen for knocking or rattling and watch the exhaust for smoke.

On the test drive

Drive at both city and highway speeds if you can. The car should track straight with your hands light on the wheel, shift smoothly and stop without pulling, grinding or a soft pedal.

The paperwork

Make sure the VIN on the dashboard and driver's door jamb matches the title and the history report. You can check any VIN for open safety recalls at nhtsa.gov/recalls.
